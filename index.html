Urine reporting rules
  • Report ALL resistant antibiotics for urines.
  • Release Augmentin on ampicillin-resistant isolates (Winpath rule appends it automatically).
  • Release Nitrofurantoin only for E. coli — breakpoints are not validated for other coliforms, Proteus or Pseudomonas.
  • Enter results as S / R / H (highly resistant) / W (waiting). EUCAST no longer recognises “I” as Intermediate — it now means Increased exposure, and a Winpath rule appends the aminoglycoside-combination comment.
Inferred resistance — override the zone
  • ESBL / AmpC: report ALL cephalosporins resistant irrespective of zone size (cefepime excepted where AmpC-stable). Add the @ESB / @AMC / @IAC comment.
  • Inducible clindamycin (D-zone): erythromycin adjacent to clindamycin — a flattened clindamycin zone = report BOTH erythromycin and clindamycin resistant.
  • Carbapenemase (CPE): add the CPE comment, notify infection control, and refer sterile-site isolates.

SOP interpretation & reporting logic — toggle the highlighted boxes
Reporting & inferred resistance
  • S. aureus — make resistant agents reportable (Winpath rule, per CHC1139): Rifampicin, Trimethoprim, Gentamicin, Linezolid, Mupirocin, Tigecycline, Norfloxacin (except ears/eyes, where it indicates Ciprofloxacin), Septrin, Ciprofloxacin, Ampicillin, Nitrofurantoin, Fucidin, Clindamycin, Erythromycin.
  • Cefoxitin is reported as Flucloxacillin; Tetracycline is reported as Doxycycline (by rule).
  • Coliform conditional release: Augmentin only if ampicillin R; Ciprofloxacin and Pip/Taz only if ampicillin AND augmentin R.
  • ESBL / AmpC: report ALL cephalosporins resistant irrespective of zone (cefepime excepted where AmpC-stable). Proteus: report tigecycline resistant irrespective of zone. Add the relevant @ESB/@AMC/@IAC/CPE comment.
  • Inducible clindamycin (D-zone): flattening of clindamycin adjacent to erythromycin = report BOTH resistant.
Organism interpretive thresholds
  • S. pneumoniae — Oxacillin 1 µg screen (non-CSF): ≥20 mm → report all β-lactams penicillin-susceptible (cefaclor I); <20 & ≥8 mm → Amp/Amox/Pip/CTX/CRO susceptible; <8 mm → do penicillin MIC (infer to amoxicillin/piperacillin). If penicillin MIC 0.5–2 confirm by broth dilution. CSF: <20 mm = report penicillin RESISTANT — see CSF view.
  • H. influenzae — Benzylpenicillin 1 µg screen: ≥12 mm → report susceptible to all β-lactams (amoxicillin, piperacillin). <12 mm & β-lactamase POSITIVE → Amp/Amox/Pip resistant, test other intended β-lactams. <12 mm & β-lactamase NEGATIVE → test the intended β-lactam and report β-lactamase negative. (Amoxicillin/ampicillin: IE for meningitis.)
  • Staphylococci — Vancomycin: sharp zone ≥12 mm = S; fuzzy zone → confirm by MIC or report R.

SOP Table 4 interpretation & reporting logic — toggle the highlighted boxes
SEND ALL CSF results to the consultant microbiologist before authorising. CSF susceptibilities carry direct clinical-urgency and public-health weight — do not release independently. Invasive meningococcal disease is notifiable: inform the duty consultant / IPC and refer the isolate for serogrouping.
CSF interpretive rules
  • S. pneumoniae — Oxacillin 1 µg (CSF): zone <20 mm → report penicillin RESISTANT and perform Ceftriaxone & Meropenem MICs. Never report penicillin susceptible on the disc for CSF.
  • Meningitis breakpoints differ from other sites (lower) — interpret β-lactam MICs against the EUCAST meningitis column.
  • Listeria: cephalosporins are intrinsically inactive — never report them. Ampicillin (or penicillin) + gentamicin is standard.
  • H. influenzae / Listeria: Meropenem MIC for CSF is added by request only.
  • Enter results as S / R / H / W; all CSF reports are consultant-authorised.

Faeces

Culture, microscopy and investigation protocols for faecal specimens. Overview sets out what is performed and when; Investigation guide is the decision matrix by patient type; Media incubation gives plate set, atmosphere and conditions; Wet preparation covers parasitology and staining. A local SOP aid only — always confirm against your current laboratory protocol.

Routine Culture

  • To be performed on samples from community.
  • Routine culture will NOT be performed on inpatients UNLESS admission was less than 3 days ago.
  • Culture to include XLD Salmonella / Shigella, CAMP Campylobacter and SMAC E. coli O157.

Microscopy

For white blood cells, red blood cells, ova, cysts and parasites. Performed if:

  • Liquid samples
  • Suspected parasites
  • Foreign travel

Adenovirus / Rotavirus

Performed on children 5 years and less.

Cryptosporidium

Performed on:

  • Liquid samples
  • Immunosuppressed
  • Children 16 years old or less
  • Foreign travel

C. difficile

Refer to PTD-64 C. difficile for full details. Performed on:

  • GP request
  • Community patients >65 years with diarrhoeal stool
  • Inpatients >2 years old
  • Antibiotic associated diarrhoea, pseudomembranous colitis

NOT performed on formed stools.

Outbreak

  • Healthcare institution (hospital ward / nursing home) outbreak will be investigated for Norovirus & C. difficile.
  • Community outbreak will be investigated for Salmonella, Shigella, Campylobacter and E. coli O157 plus additional pathogens as indicated.

Foreign Travel

Routine culture and Cryptosporidium plus investigation for Vibrio species and microscopy for parasites if travel to endemic area. Investigation for additional organisms on request.

Additional Investigations

Culture specifically for pathogens such as Aeromonas sp, Yersinia sp. This will be dependent on clinical details provided such as mesenteric adenitis or upon specific request.

Infection control should notify the lab of outbreaks and send a list of affected patients to the laboratory. Any requests that mention Outbreak require verification by a Team Manager with infection control before being treated as such. Details should be documented on Outbreak record sheet PF-LF-361 and kept in the outbreak folder until the results become available.
  • Process as per the investigation guide. Perform other investigations when specifically requested.
  • Do not process more than one sample per patient for each outbreak.
  • Only process the first six samples of an outbreak.
  • Once the aetiological agent from an outbreak has been identified, subsequent samples should just be treated as routine samples and reported with coded comment @XFLU (states that no further samples will be sent to the Ref lab from this outbreak).
  • If Norovirus has been identified as the aetiological agent from a particular source, further investigation for Noro should not be carried out until 1 month has elapsed. Do not process – report with coded comment @XNOR.
  • Norovirus samples are now tested in house on the Genexpert.

Containment: All work on suspected Salmonella typhi, Salmonella paratyphi A, B or C, Shigella dysenteriae, Vibrio cholerae and E. coli O157 must be carried out in the Category 3 room in the Biological Safety Cabinet (BSC).

Wet Preparation (with reference to the Parasitology SOP)

Perform on liquid, gassy, blood-stained or mucoid samples. Also perform if OCP performed and/or foreign travel outside Europe.

Direct Wet Prep

  • Prepare a wet preparation on a clean microscope slide by applying a small amount of patient sample using the swab that was used to inoculate the selenite F broth and covering with a coverslip.
  • The wet prep slide can be kept in a humid chamber for reading by the SBMS to prevent drying.

Stained Wet Prep

  • Motile trophozoites can be examined in fresh samples by placing one drop of 0.85% saline on the left-hand side of a clean microscope slide and one drop of double-strength Lugol's iodine on the right-hand side (the distance between the drops should be sufficient to enable coverslips to be placed over each drop).
  • Using a different swab stick for each preparation, take a small amount of unfixed faeces and thoroughly emulsify in the saline and in the Lugol's iodine.
  • Place cover slips over each preparation on the slide. Examine entire slide using a low power objective.
  • Use a medium power objective to identify any suspicious morphological features.

Faecal Concentrate Method

Refer to Parasitology SOP PF-MIC-LP-76 for details on the faecal concentrate method (the formol-ether* concentration technique).

Auramine Stain for Cryptosporidium Oocysts

  • To prepare a smear for Cryptosporidium staining, make a medium thick smear of faeces on a clean microscope slide using a swab. (Refer to Parasitology SOP PF-MIC-LP-76 and PF-MIC-LP-123 staining procedures for further details.)

Field Stain or Ziehl Neelsen Stain

  • If required, prepare a medium thick smear on a clean microscope slide for Ziehl Neelsen or Giemsa staining (refer to Parasitology SOP PF-MIC-LP-76 and PF-MIC-LP-123 staining procedures for further details).
